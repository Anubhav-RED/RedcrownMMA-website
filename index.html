TITLE: BJJ in Delhi NCR — How to Verify Your Coach Before You Sign Up DATE: 2026-06-24 CATEGORY: BJJ SLUG: bjj-delhi-ncr-verify-coach-credentials EXCERPT: Delhi NCR's BJJ scene has a fake credentials problem. Here is exactly how to check whether your coach's belt is real before you pay a rupee. --- Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu has a lineage problem in India, and Delhi NCR is ground zero for it. Unlike boxing or Muay Thai, where a skilled eye can broadly assess technical quality from watching a class, BJJ has a belt system that is supposed to guarantee a verifiable chain of instruction. A Blue Belt means you received that belt from a Purple Belt or above. A Purple Belt means the same. All the way up to Black Belt, every rank is supposed to trace back to a legitimate source. The problem is that in Delhi NCR, nobody checks. ## What "World Class Instructors" Actually Tells You The next time you visit a martial arts gym in this region, look at how they describe their coaching staff. If you see phrases like "world class instructors," "certified trainers," or "trained in MMA and BJJ" without a single name, rank, or lineage attached — that should be your first question. Vague credentials aren't a marketing choice. They're a strategy. A coach who holds a genuine BJJ belt from a legitimate instructor is proud to tell you exactly who gave it to them. They'll name their instructor, name their instructor's instructor, and show you where that chain leads. BJJ lineage is a point of pride in the legitimate grappling community — it connects you to a tradition of technique that traces back to the Gracie family in Brazil. A coach who pivots to talking about "years of experience" and "passionate teaching" when you ask about their belt has answered your question without answering it. ## The Specific Questions to Ask When you visit any BJJ program in Delhi NCR — Noida, Gurgaon, South Delhi, wherever — ask these questions directly: **"What belt are you, and who gave it to you?"** The answer should be immediate and specific. Name of instructor, their rank, where they trained. Not a pause, not a deflection to the program structure. **"Can you show me your lineage?"** Legitimate BJJ practitioners can map their lineage back through their instructor tree. Most will happily do it. This isn't an aggressive question — in the BJJ world it's completely normal. **"How long were you a white belt, and where did you train during that time?"** The typical progression from white to blue belt in BJJ is 1 to 3 years of consistent training. From blue to purple is another 2 to 4 years. Anyone claiming a Purple Belt with under 5 years of total training deserves a follow-up question. **"Do you compete or have you competed?"** A coach doesn't need to be an active competitor to be a good teacher. But someone who has never entered a tournament, never tested their technique against a stranger who wants to win, is coaching from a narrower perspective than someone who has. ## What Verified Lineage Looks Like At Redcrown MMA, Coach Aditya Heera holds a BJJ Blue Belt with a traceable lineage — meaning he received his belt from a named instructor whose own credentials can be verified. If you ask him who gave him his belt, he'll tell you. If you ask where that instructor trained, he'll tell you that too. This is what legitimate BJJ looks like. Not a marketing claim. A documentable chain. ## Why This Matters Beyond Credentials You might be thinking: does a Blue Belt versus a fake rank actually change what I learn on the mat? In the short term, often not visibly. Someone can teach you a triangle choke from a YouTube video and the choke will work. The problem appears over time. Technical precision in BJJ compounds. Wrong fundamentals — hip position, grip angle, weight distribution — create bad habits that take years to unlearn. A coach who learned incorrectly teaches incorrectly. By the time you realise something is structurally off in your game, you've spent 18 months ingraining the wrong movement pattern. You're not just paying for access to a mat. You're investing your time, your body, and your technique into someone else's knowledge base. That knowledge base should be verifiable. ## The Bottom Line Delhi NCR's martial arts scene is growing. There are legitimate coaches here — people with real training, real lineage, and real competition records. There are also people who watched enough fights, learned enough vocabulary, and opened a gym. Ask the hard question before you sign anything. It takes thirty seconds and it will tell you everything you need to know. If a coach is offended that you asked about their credentials, you have your answer.
